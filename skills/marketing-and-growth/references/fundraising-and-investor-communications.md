# Fundraising and investor communications

## Maintain one factual model

Use a shared source of truth for company description, market assumptions, traction, revenue, pipeline, runway, milestones, financing terms, and use of funds. Reconcile figures and dates across decks, memos, models, applications, data rooms, outreach, and updates.

Label actuals, estimates, forecasts, and scenarios. State the period, unit, currency, definition, and source for material metrics. Explain changes in definitions or cohorts. Never invent traction, customer commitments, investor interest, or market evidence.

## Match the communication to the decision

An investor asset should make it possible to evaluate the problem, solution, market, differentiation, evidence, business model, team, risks, plan, and ask. Adjust depth to the format while keeping the underlying facts consistent.

For outreach, establish relevance before asking for time. Personalize from verifiable investment focus or prior work, not superficial biography. Keep the ask specific and make follow-up cadence respectful. Warm-introduction text should be easy for the intermediary to forward without rewriting.

## Quality gate

Check calculation ties, timeline consistency, claim support, confidentiality, permissions, and disclosure obligations. Flag legal, securities, tax, or accounting questions for qualified review. Persuasive framing must not obscure material risk or uncertainty.
