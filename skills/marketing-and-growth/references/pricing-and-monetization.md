# Pricing and monetization

## Model value before price

Identify who pays, what outcome creates value, which usage or capability tracks that value, and what alternatives set the buyer's reference point. Distinguish the value metric from the billing unit and the package boundary.

Evaluate a pricing change across:

- customer comprehension and predictability;
- value capture and contribution economics;
- segment fit and willingness to pay;
- upgrade path and expansion behavior;
- sales, support, billing, tax, and migration complexity;
- abuse, bill shock, and exclusion risks.

## Design packages deliberately

Each package should serve a recognizable use case. Gate features or capacity where the distinction reflects value or cost, not merely because a feature is popular. Avoid arbitrary tier proliferation. Make limits, overages, renewal terms, and downgrade consequences explicit.

## Test without misleading customers

Use interviews, choice research, sales evidence, behavioral data, and controlled experiments as complementary signals. Do not treat stated willingness to pay as equivalent to purchase behavior. For live tests, define the eligible population, success metric, churn or support guardrails, duration, and treatment of existing customers.

Price changes require a migration plan: notice, grandfathering or transition policy, contract handling, billing verification, customer support, and rollback constraints. Report uncertainty rather than manufacturing precision.
