# Conversion and lifecycle

## Diagnose the flow

Map the user state, intent, promise, required effort, uncertainty, and next action at each step. Inspect quantitative drop-off and qualitative evidence before redesigning. A low conversion rate can reflect weak traffic, offer, trust, product value, or measurement—not merely poor page layout.

## Reduce the right friction

Remove effort that does not protect the user or business. Keep friction required for informed consent, security, qualification, or irreversible commitments. Ask only for information needed at that stage and explain unfamiliar requirements.

For pages, forms, signup, onboarding, prompts, and paywalls:

- make the value and next action understandable;
- preserve context and progress;
- reveal costs, limits, and consequences before commitment;
- use evidence appropriate to the claim;
- provide recovery from errors and interrupted flows;
- avoid obstruction, coercion, false urgency, preselected consent, or inaccessible controls.

## Optimize the lifecycle

Define activation as an evidenced behavior associated with receiving value, not completion of an arbitrary checklist. Prioritize time to first value, then repeated value. Trigger upgrade or retention messages from relevant user state, and cap pressure that can damage trust.

Experiment on a named hypothesis. Define eligibility, assignment, primary outcome, guardrails, minimum duration, instrumentation checks, and segment analysis before reading the result. Do not optimize a local step at the expense of retention, support burden, refunds, or user welfare.
