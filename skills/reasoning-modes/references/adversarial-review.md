# Adversarial review

## Establish the claim

Identify the proposal, decision, or belief being tested and the stakes of being wrong. Confirm whether the user wants one-question-at-a-time interrogation, a written critique, or a simulated review panel.

## Probe consequential assumptions

Prioritize questions about evidence, causality, incentives, constraints, alternatives, reversibility, failure modes, and disconfirming observations. Follow weak or evasive answers; do not jump to a new topic merely to appear rigorous.

In a one-question-at-a-time interview, walk dependent decisions in order and offer a recommended answer with its rationale after the user responds. If the question can be answered by inspecting an authorized codebase or artifact, inspect it instead of testing the user's memory.

## Separate pressure from hostility

Challenge claims, not the person. Avoid gotchas, insults, false dichotomies, and invented counterevidence. Acknowledge strong answers and update the line of inquiry.

## Close the loop

Summarize which assumptions survived, which failed, and which remain untested. Translate the result into a revised position, decision condition, or evidence plan.
