# Creative direction

## Frame the experience

Capture four anchors before styling:

- audience and context of use;
- primary job and desired action;
- emotional register, such as assured, warm, urgent, technical, or playful;
- one differentiating idea that can be expressed through composition, type, imagery, or interaction.

Turn them into a single sentence: “For [audience doing job], the interface should feel [register] through [specific visual idea].” Use that sentence to reject incoherent choices.

## Select a profile

Choose one profile or define an equally specific alternative:

- **Restrained utility:** dense clarity, quiet hierarchy, minimal motion, high information efficiency.
- **Editorial:** expressive typography, deliberate rhythm, strong art direction, asymmetric composition where content supports it.
- **Playful system:** vivid feedback, approachable shape language, controlled surprise, clear recovery from experimentation.
- **Technical/industrial:** exposed structure, precise grids, functional contrast, compact data presentation.
- **Premium:** disciplined whitespace, excellent imagery, subtle material cues, fewer but stronger moments.

Profiles are starting constraints, not templates.

## Make hierarchy observable

For each region, identify what should be noticed first, what action follows, and what can remain quiet. Create hierarchy through a coordinated use of scale, contrast, position, grouping, and whitespace. Do not rely on font weight alone.

## Use motion with a job

Motion should explain spatial change, acknowledge input, establish continuity, or focus attention. Prefer a small motion vocabulary. Avoid decorative movement that delays comprehension, and provide a reduced-motion path.
