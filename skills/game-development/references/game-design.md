# Game design

## Define the loop

Describe what the player perceives, decides, does, and receives on a short repeating cadence. State the skill or understanding that improves over time. If the loop is not engaging with placeholder content, more content is unlikely to repair it.

## Design mechanics as decisions

For each mechanic, identify its inputs, state, feedback, tradeoffs, interactions, and failure cases. Remove mechanics that add execution without meaningful choice. Teach through safe exposure, escalating variation, and readable consequences.

## Tune challenge and progression

Balance difficulty around intended player learning, not only numeric growth. Introduce complexity in layers, preserve viable recovery, and avoid progress systems that invalidate the core play. Model economies with sources, sinks, pacing, and exploit paths.

## Playtest hypotheses

Write a question for each session. Observe behavior before explaining. Record where players hesitate, misread feedback, discover exploits, or create unexpected strategies. Separate preference from comprehension and performance evidence.

## Support diverse players

Provide remappable controls, readable presentation, adjustable audio, difficulty or assistance options appropriate to the design, and alternatives to color-, timing-, or motion-only information.
