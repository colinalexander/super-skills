# Workplace writing

## Write for the decision

Identify audience, purpose, desired action, sensitivity, and channel. Lead with the outcome or decision. Put context, evidence, risks, and next steps in the order the reader needs them.

## Use appropriate forms

- **Status update:** outcome, evidence, risks/blockers, owner, next milestone.
- **Decision memo:** decision, rationale, alternatives, implications, follow-up.
- **FAQ:** real reader questions with direct, non-duplicative answers.
- **Incident update:** confirmed facts, impact, mitigation, next update; distinguish hypotheses.
- **Internal announcement:** what changes, who is affected, when, required action, help path.

## Coauthor safely

Preserve the author's voice and distinguish substantive edits from copy edits. Resolve contradictions instead of smoothing them over. Track open decisions and source gaps. Do not invent consensus, dates, owners, or metrics.

For a substantial draft, predict the questions a reader will ask, then conduct a fresh-context reader pass that has access only to the artifact and its intended audience—not the drafting conversation. Repair gaps that force the reader to infer critical context. Treat this as a comprehension test, not automatic permission to change the author's position.

## Finish for the channel

Remove throat-clearing, duplicated conclusions, and unexplained jargon. Check names, dates, links, and numerical consistency. Use headings and bullets only when they improve scanning.
